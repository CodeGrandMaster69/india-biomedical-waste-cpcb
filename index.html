Priority states for a new CBWTF

Score = 50% capacity shortfall (2024) + 30% untreated waste (2020–24 total) + 20% years with a gap.

Recommendation

  1. Kerala: add capacity. 2 CBWTFs serve 1.42 lakh beds (71,089 beds each, 5.6× the national ratio) and ~97 t/day. It reports full treatment, so the risk is overload and single-point failure, not an open gap.
  2. Bihar: build and verify. 37–65% of waste was treated in 2020–23. It then reported 100% in 2024 with the same 4 facilities. Treat that jump as unverified until an audit confirms it.
  3. Assam: fix a persistent gap. The only state that left more than 12% of its waste untreated every year. The volume is small (~7 t/day), so one additional facility could close it.
  4. Watch Manipur and Puducherry. Each reported 20–30% untreated in 2024.

Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan and West Bengal sit 4–7 facilities below the trend line but report full treatment. Their need is headroom, not an emergency.

Data caveats for the selected year
    Source: Central Pollution Control Board, Annual Report on Biomedical Waste Management 2020–2024, Annexure-I (self-reported by State Pollution Control Boards). 2020–21 exclude COVID-19 waste, which was reported only as a national total. "Untreated" is generated minus treated, floored at zero per state. DGAFMS (Armed Forces) is in national totals but not on the map. Map: state boundaries as depicted by the Government of India.
